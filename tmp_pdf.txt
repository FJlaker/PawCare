INTRODUCTION 
 
Scenario: Ward 42 Fix-It, a community service-reporting web app 
The Mamelodi East Residents’ Association is a volunteer organisation that works with the ward councillor’s 
office in Ward 42, Tshwane. Every week residents report potholes, burst water pipes, broken streetlights and 
illegal dumping. At present these report s arrive as WhatsApp voice notes, photos and phone calls to one 
volunteer coordinator, Mr Tshepo Mahlangu. Reports get lost, residents do not know whether anything is 
being done, and the councillor’s office cannot see which problems are most common. 
The association has received a small community grant and has asked you, a junior web developer at Mzansi 
Digital Labs (a small software company in Pretoria), to build Ward 42 Fix-It: a web application that residents 
can use on their phones to log a fault with a photo, and that the coordinator can use to manage and close 
reports. 
What the association needs 
• Residents can submit a report with a category, a street name, a short description and an optional photo, 
and can then look up the status of their report by its reference number. 

 
WST262 SS3 – Summative Project: Ward 42 Fix-It  Page 2 of 13 
• The coordinator can log in, see all reports in a table, filter them by category and status, update a report’s 
status (Open, In progress, Closed), and view a simple dashboard that counts reports by category and 
status. 
• A short tutorial video on the home page shows residents how to submit a report. 
• Mobile and low-data use: most residents use entry-level Android phones on prepaid mobile data, often 
with weak signal. The app must be responsive, light on data, and installable as a Progressive Web App 
(PWA) that still shows a useful page when the phone is offline. 
• Accessibility: many residents are older people, and some use screen readers or enlarge the text on their 
phones. The app must follow the POUR principles and WCAG guidance in Topic 6 of the Study Guide. 
• Security and privacy: reports contain residents’ names, cell phone numbers and photos of their streets. 
The application must protect this personal information, in line with the Protection of Personal Information 
Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA), and must resist the vulnerabilities covered in Topic 7 of the Study Guide.
